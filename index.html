Manifesto

Manifesto for organisations that apply AI wisely

Becoming an AI-optimal organisation means reaching results faster because you act smarter, not by chasing new technology without a goal or a measure. We evolve the organisation into a network of value flows where people and AI work together, and AI is applied exactly where and as far as it creates measurable value. While others chase the hype and then redo their work, we take the shorter path to real, measurable efficiency, without stopping the business for a single day.

Smart organisation

  1. 01Not more AI. More value you can see.The goal is not maximum AI or the most tools, but a measurable result: savings or revenue you can count and feel. AI is a tool for the organisation’s success, not an end in itself. Each task is automated exactly as far as value, risk, cost and the law justify. This discipline of fit is what makes us faster than those who automate everything.
  2. 02Continuous improvement, built into the work.Change is not a one-off project but a built-in mode: the organisation keeps reviewing and improving its value chains without stopping daily work. The improvement cycle lives in the operating fabric itself, so change becomes the norm, not a shock. We build security, reliability and compliance into that change by design, before an incident, not after. And every cycle makes the organisation faster, cheaper and stronger.

Human strength

  1. 03People lead, AI acts.People are neither victims of change nor the link left over after automation: they lead the transformation. People decide what to delegate to AI, set limits and controls, oversee its work and stay accountable for the result. We carry out change together with people, explain honestly what is changing and why, and help everyone find a role where their judgement matters most.
  2. 04Growing tomorrow’s experts today.The biggest risk of automation is not lost jobs but a lost path by which masters grow: hand all entry-level work to agents, and tomorrow the organisation may have no specialists able to be accountable for AI. So we deliberately preserve and grow that source now, developing people towards judgement, evaluation and oversight of AI’s work rather than competing with it on execution.

Shared intelligence

  1. 05Shared context, end to end.The value an organisation delivers reaches its recipient through the hands of many people, departments and agents. AI should help keep one context along the whole chain, so information is not lost or distorted at hand-offs. We work in a shared context instead of passing each other fragments, and that is what makes shared intelligence possible.
  2. 06The best emerges through interaction.Shared intelligence is not only human + AI but also human + human. Trust, mentoring, hard conversations and joint decisions are born between people; AI amplifies this but does not replace it. The best decisions emerge where the synergy of people and agents gives more than the sum of its parts.
Principles

Twelve principles

The principles turn the manifesto into rules for action. The first three apply to everything.

Conditions for everything

  1. Measurable value before launch.
  2. Security and compliance by design.
  3. No data foundation, no AI on the flow.

Keep pace without losing control

  1. Change is a permanent safe mode, not a project.
  2. Autonomy is granted step by step, together with control.
  3. A named person is accountable for every agent flow.

Automate without losing people

  1. Roles shift towards judgement; they don't disappear.
  2. Keep control of the path by which experts grow.
  3. Transform at the speed of conscious adoption.

Prepare the organisation, not just the technology

  1. AI is part of the workflow, not an add-on.
  2. The sanctioned path must be easier than the shadow one.
  3. Value has an owner and an assessor.
Maturity model

Maturity is the quality of the decision

Two companies can look identical: some work automated, some done by hand. One is at level 1, the other at level 5. The difference is whether a deliberate, recorded decision stands behind it.

L1Chaos

AI is used spontaneously, without control. Risk outweighs benefit.

No decision: things just happened.

L2Control

A safe foundation: rules, approved tools, data under control, basic AI literacy and a working AI Centre.

Decisions are about what is allowed and safe, not yet what is worthwhile.

L3Smart tasks

Competence centres have the process and infrastructure to prioritise tasks in their areas and set the right depth of automation for each. The AI Centre supports them with method and technology.

Weighed and recorded, task by task.

L4Smart flows

The same for whole flows: areas are stitched into an end-to-end flow without losing context, and the team reassesses which tasks to keep, merge or remove.

Weighed for the whole flow, including whether every task is needed.

L5Built-in maturity

Fit reproduces itself: new flows are designed optimal from the start, and existing ones are continuously reviewed and improved.

Made up front and reviewed regularly.

Roles

One new body. Two new focuses.

WAAI splits AI responsibility three ways. A "centre" is a role, not necessarily a department. In a small company one person can hold more than one.

Existing leadership + new focus

Strategic Centre

Sets direction and how far the transformation should go, funds it, and decides which flows matter most.

In a flow team: Strategy Owner, accountable for the flow's value.

Existing experts + new focus

Competence Centres

Heads of each area decide what to hand to AI in their field, how deep, and which checks stay human.

In a flow team: Competence Owners, one per area of the flow.

Genuinely new capability

AI Centre

Owns tools, security, training and shared practices. It teaches experts to lead improvements rather than doing it for them.

In a flow team: Flow Architect, who keeps context from getting lost between areas.
